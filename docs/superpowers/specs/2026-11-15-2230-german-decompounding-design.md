# Trending: German decompounding (#2230)

## Intent

Since #2227, Trending stems a German entry's words with the Snowball German stemmer. Snowball does not split
compounds, so the links the six-character cut made by accident between a compound and its parts are gone:
*Spionage / Spionageverdacht*, *Polizei / Polizeieinsatz*, *Cyberangriff / Cyberattacke*, *Dreiergipfel /
Dreiertreffen*, *Sonntagmittag / Sonntagvormittag*. The #2227 Outcome measured German-to-German grouped pairs as a net
loss over the week (lost 741, gained 541). A heuristic put compounds at up to about 255 week pairs and 35 day pairs.
Verb forms ending in *-t*, which Snowball leaves alone (*verhandelt / verhandeln*, *kündigt / kündigen*), account for
part of the rest (the inflection class, 306 week pairs, mostly German).

This change splits German compounds into their parts with a lexicon that does not depend on the news, and joins the
*-t* verb forms to their infinitive by rule. Both rules are general: no word list is curated, from the window or by hand.

## Goals

- A German word that is a compound of known words yields its own stem plus the stems of its parts.
- The whole compound stays a stem, so two entries sharing the exact compound share more than two sharing one part.
- Linking elements (*-s-*, *-es-*, *-n-*, *-en-*, *-er-*, *-e-*, *-ens-*, and the dropped *-e* of *Schul-*) are
  handled by rule.
- A German verb's *-t* and *-et* forms stem like its infinitive.
- The lexicon ships in the repo as a generated PHP data file, with its generator, source pin and licence notice.
- The lexicon loads only when Trending stems a German word.

## Non-goals

- Compounds in English, French or Spanish. English compounds are mostly written apart; French and Spanish join with
  prepositions.
- Derivation beyond the *-t* rule: *Kandidatur / kandidiert*, *Verhandlungen / verhandeln* stay apart.
- A German lemmatizer. The verb rule maps two inflection endings; it does not lemmatize.
- Any change to the #2225/#2227 tables, `HeadlineFiller` shares, link thresholds or the reader-facing API.
- The other #2227 proposals (Snowball stopword lists, CLDR demonyms, the proper-noun guard). They are separate issues.

## Anti-goals

- **Wrong merges through common parts.** *Land*, *Haus*, *Tag* or *Bund* must not link two unrelated stories by
  themselves. The eye check in the measurement looks for exactly this, and a group joined by a part alone blocks the PR.
- **A curated list.** No part, no stop-part and no split comes from the current window or from a hand-made list.
  The window validates the mechanism only.
- **Week regression.** As in #2227, the week window must not lose real stories net.
- **A licence the MIT repo cannot carry.** No GPL-only, no CC BY-SA, no data whose licence could not be confirmed.

## Constraints

- PHP 8.5 and Symfony 8.1 on a shared host (Strato). No system binary at runtime (no hunspell, no Python).
- The data ships as a PHP data file under `backend/resources/`, generated by a committed script. The precedent is
  `backend/resources/feed-directory/` (`snapshot.php`, `SOURCE`, `ABOUT.txt`, `LICENSE.txt`).
- Budget for the data, fixed now and not tuned against the window:
  - file at most 6 MB;
  - including it raises memory by at most 40 MB and takes at most 0.5 s without opcache (the worker runs in CLI, where
    opcache is usually off);
  - the week run's wall time grows by at most 15 % against the same code without decompounding.
- Unreleased data needs no migration, and this change needs no schema change at all.
- House rules (CLAUDE.md): an interface sits in a folder named after it, with its implementations; a per-call object
  built with `new` sits in `Pass/`; a stateful service implements `ResetInterface` or carries
  `#[ProcessLifetimeState]`. Every touched `src` file is PHPMD-clean, with no suppressions and no count-driven splits.
- Lands after #2227 (PR #2232). It needs `entry.language` and `feed.language` and the per-language stemming.

The Ngram data is noisy (OCR) and large (several GB compressed for German). The generator streams it once and keeps
only what fits the budget. Its noise is mostly rare forms, which a frequency floor removes.

### The splitting algorithm

1. **Longest known head, greedy.** Cheap, but it cannot tell *Staubecken* (*Stau+Becken* or *Staub+Ecken*) and will
   split frequent non-compounds (*Aktion = Akt + Ion*).
2. **Lexicon recursion, scored by part frequency** (Koehn & Knight 2003). Enumerate every split into known parts. The
   unsplit word is a candidate too, ranked by its own frequency. Choose the candidate whose parts have the highest
   geometric-mean frequency. A frequent simple word (*Aktion*) beats its rare "parts", and a compound (*Polizeieinsatz*)
   loses to its frequent parts. No tuned value: the score uses only the lexicon's own frequencies.
3. **A trained character model** (CharSplit-style). Rejected with its data.

**Chosen: 2**, with the linking elements by rule. Ties go to fewer parts, then to the longer head.

### How the parts enter Trending

1. **Replace the compound by its parts.** The exact compound would count no more than the parts. Rejected (the issue
   keeps the whole).
2. **Whole plus parts, every part at the word's place weight** (headline 1.0, body 0.5). Each part's rarity comes from
   its own document frequency in the window, like any stem. This is the issue's proposal.
3. **Whole plus parts at a reduced weight.** It protects the share-of-the-lighter rule (below), but it would put a new
   constant into the weighting before any measurement shows the need.

**Chosen: 2.** Here is why it should hold. `HeadlineWindow` weighs a stem by `log((N + 2) / (df + 1))`, and a stem in
3 % or more of the (floored) window is filler and weighs nothing. `HeadlineLinks` needs a shared weight of 14 and
40 % of the lighter entry's total. A common part such as *tag* (*Bundestag, Sonntag, Parteitag, Dienstag, …*) or
*land* gets a large document frequency. In the week window it is likely filler, and if not, it weighs about 3–4.
That is a quarter of the 14 a link needs, so one common part cannot link two entries by itself. The measurement checks
this assumption.

Option 2 has one real risk: **dilution**. An entry whose compound is not shared now carries two or three extra rare
stems in its total weight, so the share of the lighter entry can drop below 40 %. German entries grow heavier than the
English entries they used to match (de–en lost 128 pairs net in #2227). The measurement looks for this as well (see
"Remedies").

## Design

This section describes what was built. The approaches above, the pre-registered remedies below and the Outcome keep
the history of how it got here.

### Lexicon data

`backend/resources/german-lexicon/`:

- `generate.php` streams the 8 pinned German 1-gram shards of Ngram v3 (`gzopen`, line by line) and keeps:
  - the years 1950 to 2019;
  - alphabetic tokens (`^\p{L}+$`) of at least 3 letters, casefolded (`MB_CASE_FOLD`, NFKC), diacritics kept.
  It sums the counts per casefolded form, overall and per part-of-speech tag, and writes one row per kept form.
  - **Kind:** `n` (noun) when the form's `_NOUN`-tagged count is at least half its tagged count, otherwise `o`
    (other). The 4 kept forms without any tag fall back to the capitalised spelling's share of the untagged count.
  - **Frequency class:** `floor(log2(maxCount / count))`, with `maxCount` the count of the most frequent kept form
    (the Leipzig convention: class 0 is the most frequent, each class half as frequent as the one before).
  - **Size cap:** the 290,000 most frequent forms, the largest round number that fits the 6 MB budget.
- `lexicon.tsv` is the generated data: `form TAB n|o TAB class`, one line per form, byte-sorted, 4,799,138 bytes
  (189,565 nouns, 100,435 others). It is never edited by hand. There is no verb data and no side file.
- `SOURCE` pins the shard URLs, sizes and SHA-256s, the fetch date, the year range, N and the end-to-end
  reproduction. `NOTICE.txt` is the CC BY 3.0 attribution, `LICENSE.txt` its legal code; the repository's code stays
  MIT.

### Runtime

All of it lives in `Service/Curation/Trending`:

- **`GermanLexicon/GermanLexiconInterface`** has one method, `entryOf(string $form): ?GermanLexiconEntryModel`: the
  form's kind (`isNoun()`) and frequency class, or null for a form the lexicon lacks. One lookup gives both.
- **`GermanLexicon/BundledGermanLexicon`** reads `lexicon.tsv` into one string on the first lookup
  (`Model/GermanLexiconModel`) and finds a form by binary search over the string's byte offsets
  (`Model/SortedLinesModel`, comparing a line in place with `substr_compare`). Loading costs 1.3 ms and holds 4.6 MB,
  with no parse transient, opcache or not; a lookup costs about 10 µs. It implements `ResetInterface`, so the worker
  frees the string between messages.
- **`GermanCompoundSplitter::partsOf(string $word): list<string>`** returns the lexicon forms of the chosen split, or
  `[]`. A word needs at least `2 × MIN_PART_LENGTH` letters (`MIN_PART_LENGTH = 3`). `Pass/CompoundSplitSearch`
  memoises the best split of each suffix, so the search is linear in split points times linking variants. The rules:
  - **R1. Every part is a noun form**, the head and each modifier alike, inflected forms included (*-einsätze*). A part
    needs a vowel. Function words, adjectives and verb stems are never modifiers, so *Groß-stadt* and *Fahr-zeug* do
    not split.
  - **Linking elements by rule.** A modifier segment `m` resolves to the most frequent noun among `m`, `m−s`, `m−es`,
    `m−n`, `m−en`, `m−er`, `m−e`, `m−ens` and `m+e` (the dropped *-e* of *Schul-bus*). A tie goes to the shorter form,
    the bare segment.
  - **R2. Fewest parts win;** a tie goes to the lower mean frequency class, then to the longer head. A word the lexicon
    knows stays whole when its own class is at or below the best split's mean class (Koehn & Knight's comparison).
  - **R4. A known word splits only into parts at least as frequent as itself**: a compound is never more common than
    its parts.
  - **R7. A word the lexicon lacks splits into at most 2 parts**; a known word into at most `MAX_PARTS = 4`.
  - **No class floor for parts.** The per-length floors (median, weighted median, 99th percentile) were measured inert
    or wrong and removed; only the vowel rule is left.
- **`HeadlineEquivalents::wordStemsOf()`** gives the whole word's stem first, then one stem per part, each part
  stemmed through Snowball and then the German equivalence map (*Russlandreise* yields `russia`). A word that is a
  canonical of the equivalence tables is never split. Duplicates are removed.
- **`HeadlineStemmer`** is unchanged: there is no verb rule (Task 7 dropped it; see the Outcome).
- **`Pass/HeadlineWindow`** caches each word's stem id and, for a compound, its part ids, per language and word.
  An entry's stems are its words' stems plus their parts' stems; a part that is a seed drops out, and a part that is
  also a word of the entry counts as that word. `Model/EntryStemIdsModel` holds an entry's headline stems, the body
  stems the headline lacks, and the stems it holds only as a compound's parts.

### How the parts enter a pair

A part weighs like any stem: its place weight (headline 1.0, body 0.5) times its rarity in the window, and it is
filler when any stem would be. An entry's total weight leaves out the stems it holds only as parts, so a compound
counts once in the total. `Pass/CompoundParts` keeps which compounds each entry holds (with their place weight) and
which stems each entry holds only as parts. Two rules govern what the parts add to a pair:

- **R6. A part counts toward a pair only where the pair does not share the whole compound.** In each entry that holds
  the stem only as a part, the stem is dropped from the pair when the other entry holds every compound it comes from.
  The parts link *different* words (*Polizeieinsatz* ↔ *Polizei*, *Polizeieinsatz* ↔ *Feuerwehreinsatz*); a shared
  compound weighs once. Which stems are compounds is settled for the whole window: when a later word first splits a
  stem, the entries already holding that stem are marked as holding the compound, so the result does not depend on the
  order the entries arrive in.
- **R8. Within one pair, a compound adds through its parts at most its own weight** (its place weight times its
  rarity). The parts' weights are scaled down together until they fit; a part coming through several compounds is
  held to the tightest of their caps (`Support/PartWeightCaps`). So *Polizeieinsatz* ↔ *Einsatz der Polizei* shares at
  most r(*polizeieinsatz*), and "share of the lighter entry" stays a share.

### Cost, as built (E, Outcome below)

Against develop (A) at the same pin, the week run takes 66.2 s against 65.3 s (+1.4 %) and peaks at 176.3 MB of PHP
memory against 147.3 MB (+29 MB; RSS 246.5 MB against 239.6 MB). The day run takes 4.5 s against 3.6 s and peaks at
54.3 MB against 46.3 MB. The lexicon is 4.6 MB of the difference; the rest is the part postings, the `CompoundParts`
bookkeeping and the explain command's extra near misses.

### The stem cache stays per run (ruling)

The cache key is the language and the word, and the value depends on nothing else. A process-wide cache would never be
reset in the long-running worker and would grow with every new word. A per-run cache is bounded by one window's
vocabulary and is dropped with the `HeadlineWindow`. The expensive part that does live across words, the lexicon, is
cached separately and lazily by `BundledGermanLexicon`.

### Remedies, fixed before the measurement

Registered before any measurement and kept as registered; the Outcome reports how each one fired.


Each remedy is written down now so that the measurement cannot tune one:

- **Wrong merges through a common part.** This applies if the eye check finds a new or grown group whose members share
  only a part stem with no story in common. The remedy is a **part-frequency floor**: a part in the lexicon's most
  frequent 1 % of noun forms (frequency class at or below the class at that quantile). If the remedy applies, the generator
  computes the class and stores it in `lexicon.php` as `'commonPartClass'`. adds no stem. The 1 % is fixed here, and the window does not move it. If wrong merges remain
  after the floor, report them and stop. Do not tighten it.
- **Dilution.** This applies if German-to-German or de–en grouped pairs go net negative against A, and the near misses
  failing "share of the lighter entry" rise by more than the pairs gained. The remedy is parts at the body ratio, half
  the word's place weight. That ratio already exists in `HeadlineWindow`, so it is not a new number. It is measured
  once, and the better of the two is reported.
- **The verb rule.** It ships only if its own A/B (B2 against B1) is not net negative on the week and the eye check
  finds no wrong merge through it. Otherwise it is dropped, not tuned.

## Limitations

Known and accepted; none of them is fixed by a list.

- **Junk modifiers of known words.** R7 limits only words the lexicon lacks, so a known word can still split into
  short rare nouns: *Bundesfinanzhof* gives *bund + fin + anz + hof*. R8 limits what such parts add to a pair (the
  Task 7b *Bundesbank* / *Bundesfinanzhof* merge is gone in E), but the parts still exist and can link.
- **An inflection can split where its base form does not.** *Weihnachtsbaum* is frequent enough to stay whole (R2),
  while *Weihnachtsbäume* splits into *weihnacht + bäume*. Both stem to `weihnachtsbaum`, so the stem counts as a
  compound in every entry that holds it, and R6 treats the two spellings alike whatever order they arrive in. But only
  the plural gives the parts, so only it links to *Weihnachten* or *Baum*.
- **German parts reach cross-language canonicals.** A part goes through the equivalence map like any word, so
  *Natogipfel* gives `nato` and can link an English *NATO* or a French *OTAN* headline. This is the equivalence table
  doing its job on a part, and it is accepted; the non-German changes it causes are small.
- **A compound and its hyphenated spelling share at most the compound's weight** (R8). *Spionageverdacht* ↔
  *Spionage-Verdacht* or *Ökodiktatur* ↔ *Öko-Diktatur* share r(compound), not r(part) + r(part), so two short
  headlines that share nothing else may no longer reach the weight of 14 together. Measured in E as part of R8's cost.
- **Genre merges through two common parts.** Accident reports (*fahr*, *unfall* with *verletzt*, *schwer*, *zwei*)
  and air-travel stories (*flug*, *verkehr*) still group by kind rather than by story when nothing else separates them.
- ***Macht* joins *machen*.** The lexicon's majority vote tags the casefolded *macht* as a verb form, and Snowball
  gives both the same stem. The general fix is case-aware stemming, which #2231's proper-noun guard needs anyway; it
  moves there.

## Rulings

Lars was away, so each is my call:

1. **Data source: Google Books Ngram v3 German 1-grams, CC BY 3.0.** Hunspell `de_DE` is ruled out by its licence:
   the GPL, and an OASIS alternative that forbids modification and use outside ODF office software.
2. **Splitting is frequency-scored recursion** (Koehn & Knight), with linking elements by rule, `MIN_PART_LENGTH = 3`
   and `MAX_PARTS = 4`. The head must be a noun, and the unsplit word competes on its own frequency.
3. **Parts weigh like any word** at their place weight, and the whole compound stays. Rarity is trusted to quiet
   common parts, and the measurement checks that.
4. **The *-t* verb rule is in scope** behind its own A/B, with a noun guard. #2227 measured the inflection class at
   306 week pairs, which is enough to matter.
5. **The stem cache stays per run.** The lexicon is lazy, and freed by `ResetInterface` between worker messages.
6. **The lexicon is a PHP data file** (`return [...]`), not JSON. It loads as fast as PHP can, opcache can share it
   where it is on, and it needs no decode step.
7. **The size cap N comes from the 6 MB budget**, the years are 1950–2019, and the noun test is a majority of the
   tagged count. All three are fixed by the data's shape, not by the window.
8. **The remedies and their single fixed values (1 %, half weight) are registered above**, before any measurement.
9. **Tests use a small fixture lexicon** of general words (`tests/Fixtures/german-lexicon/lexicon.php`), so the
   existing Trending tests do not depend on the 6 MB file. One integration test reads the real file for its shape and
   for the issue's example compounds. If such an example does not split, that is a finding, never a reason to edit the
   data.

Later rulings superseded two of these: the verb rule (4) was measured and dropped (Outcome, Remedies), and the lexicon
(6) became the sorted text file `lexicon.tsv`, read by binary search, after Task 2's review found the PHP array's parse
cost too high without opcache; the fixture (9) followed as `tests/Fixtures/german-lexicon/lexicon.tsv`. The splitting
and pairing rules R1–R8 in the Design came out of the builds and measurements.

### Remedies

- **The *-t* verb rule fired its registered drop, and it is reverted** (`fix(#2230): drop the -t verb rule…`). Its own
  A/B is net positive on the week (+110), but the eye check found wrong merges carried by the verb alone. Each pair
  below shares nothing else that weighs:
  - *Fischereien erwarten stabile Preise* / *Interhyp erwartet kurzfristig stabile Zinsen*;
  - *Deutsche erwarten sinkenden Lebensstandard* / *Wirtschaft entwickelt sich besser als erwartet*;
  - *Welche Rolle der Baum des Jahres spielt* / *Welche Rollen haben die Spieler inne?*;
  - *Explosion zerstört Wohnhaus – vier Menschen im Krankenhaus* / *Ärzte ohne Grenzen kritisieren Zerstörung von
    Krankenhaus in Gaza*.
  The verb data (`verbs.tsv`, `isVerbStem`, `infinitiveStemOf`) from Tasks 1–2 was then unused, and Task 7b removed
  it; re-rendering `lexicon.tsv` and `lexicon-meta.php` from the committed file reproduced both byte for byte.
- **The dilution remedy fired, was measured once, and lost.** De–en went net negative on the week (−10), and the
  near misses failing "share of the lighter entry" rose by far more than the pairs gained. Week totals went from
  41,159 to 54,714, de–en from 2,015 to 2,272, de–de from 8,563 to 22,164. Variant **B3** is B1 with every stem a word
  holds only as a part at half its place weight. It came out worse everywhere:
  - week B1 → B3: net −779, de–de −774, de–other −5;
  - day B1 → B3: net −57;
  - A → B3: de–en −15, against −10 for B1.
  B1 is the better variant, so parts keep the word's place weight. The lost de–en pairs are of one kind: a German
  headline grows heavy with compound parts against a short English one (*Thunberg auf dem Klimagipfel* / *Greta
  Thunberg tells Cop31*, *Guterres*, *GTA 6*, *AMD Zen 6*).
- **The common-part floor did not fire.** The anti-goal never showed. *Bund* came out as window filler (weight 0), and
  *land* and *tag* weighed about 2–4. Of the 1,643 new week pairs that link directly, 3 link with less than 5 of
  whole-word weight and no junk part or verb rule involved. All three are fair: *Ökodiktatur* / *Öko-Diktatur*, and two winter-weather reports.

### Eye check

For each group, the check looked at every member's strongest direct link with and without the part stems
(`stems.php`, `eye.py` in the task scratchpad).

### The structural loss, and the general rule it suggests

Every wrong merge found has the same mechanism. A word both entries share counts once as the whole and again for each
part, two to four times over in all. That doubles or triples one piece of evidence, so one shared name or compound
clears the weight of 14 by itself. Rarity cannot stop this, because the whole and its parts are all rare. Junk parts
make it worse, but they are not its cause: with clean parts, *Steinmeier* and *Eishockey* triple-count as well.

Proposed general rule, adopted as ruling R6 and measured in Task 7b below: **a part counts toward a pair only where the
pair does not share the whole word.** The parts of *Polizeieinsatz* still link it to *Polizei*. A shared
*Steinmeier*, *tagesschau* or *Abschiebungen* counts once. The rule needs no value and no list. As a side effect, a
junk part could then only match across different words, which is rarer still. This needs a ruling before the PR. The
anti-goal as written (no group joined by a part alone) is met, but the wrong merges above are real.

### Task 7b: parts that do not double-count (R6), two parts for an unknown word (R7)

Two general rules, no word and no value from the window:

- **R6.** A stem that an entry holds only as a part of its compounds is dropped from a pair when, in either entry, every
  compound it comes from is held by the other entry too. The parts still link *different* words (*Polizeieinsatz* ↔
  *Polizei*, *Polizeieinsatz* ↔ *Feuerwehreinsatz*), but a shared compound weighs once. "Share of the lighter entry"
  stays consistent: an entry's total weight leaves out the stems it holds only as parts, so an entry paired with a copy of
  itself still shares 1.0. The parts no longer dilute an entry, which also answers the dilution that B3 was meant to fix.
  A word whose own stem is a seed now contributes no part stems either. The bookkeeping is `Pass/CompoundParts`.
- **R7.** A word the lexicon does not know splits into at most 2 parts; a known word keeps the limit of 4. The junk
  shreds Task 7 found (*abs+chi+bun+gen*, *regi+onal+liga*, *geld+sor+gen*) all needed 3 or more parts.

### E: the final fix wave (R6 in any order, R8)
