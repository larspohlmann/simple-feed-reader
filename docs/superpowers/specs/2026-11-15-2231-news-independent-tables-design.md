# Trending: news-independent tables (#2231)

## Intent

Trending's seed list (`HeadlineFiller::SEED_WORDS`, `SEED_STEMS`) and equivalence tables (`HeadlineEquivalents`) were
picked by hand from one week of dev-stack headlines in #2225 and #2227. What fits this week's news is dead weight in
three months, and a table of names needs a new entry for every new name. #2227's Outcome named the news-specific
entries (every Zelensky spelling, `WM` / `weltmeisterschaft`, arguably `kreml` / `kremlin`) and a general loss: a
proper name passed through two languages' stemmers (*Mike Hammer*: German `hamm`, English `hammer`), about 104 week
pairs.

This change takes the seeds from a source that does not depend on the news, deletes every table entry that is either
news-specific or measured to be worth nothing, and records why the other proposals of the issue (territory data, a
proper-noun guard, a transliteration fold) are not built.

## Goals

- The seeds are the Snowball project's stopword lists for de/en/fr/es, with a closed-class supplement for fr/es (D10),
  committed as generated data with their source pins and licences, and stemmed at construction as today's words are.
- The equivalence tables hold only what does not follow the news. Every person, event and country entry goes.
- Each proposal of the issue is decided by a measurement on the dev window, and the measurement is written down.

## Non-goals

- Generated country, capital or demonym data. Measured below: today's hand-made country and capital entries are worth
  nothing net, so a generated superset is not built (Decision D3).
- A proper-noun guard. Both designs were prototyped and measured; both lose week pairs (Decision D5).
- A transliteration fold (Decision D7).
- Any change to weights, filler shares, link thresholds, the German splitter, the stemmer, or the reader-facing API.

## Anti-goals

- **Curation from the window.** No seed, table entry or value comes from the current news. The window measures; it
  never supplies a word.
- **A week regression.** As in #2227 and #2230, the week window must not lose real stories net.
- **A licence the MIT repository cannot carry.** Only BSD, MIT, Unicode/CLDR, CC0 or CC BY data. No CC BY-SA, no GPL.

## Constraints

- PHP 8.5, Symfony 8.1, shared host (Strato): no runtime network access, no system binary.
- Data ships under `backend/resources/`, generated by a committed script with a `SOURCE` pin, as
  `backend/resources/german-lexicon/` (#2230) and `backend/resources/feed-directory/` do.
- House rules (CLAUDE.md): an interface sits in a folder named after it, with its implementations; `final readonly`
  by default; every touched `src` file PHPMD-clean, no suppressions, no count-driven splits; no comment unless a future
  reader would get the code wrong.
- Lands on `develop` after #2230 (merged as 75048da7e).

## Research

All measurements: dev MySQL, read-only, "now" pinned to 2026-11-15 00:00 UTC with the #2227 kit
(`simp-explain-at.php`, `compare.py`), each variant's files swapped into the checkout for one run and restored. The
worker keeps ingesting back-dated entries, so every comparison below is between two runs that read the same entry
counts (stated per row). The scripts and outputs are in the session scratchpad under `2231/`.

### R2. Territories

PHP intl ships ICU 79.1 data offline. `Locale::getDisplayRegion('und_XX', $language)` and the `ICUDATA-region`
resource bundle give every region code's name in de/en/fr/es (295 entries in German, macro-regions included), with
variant and short forms (`GB` → *UK*, `US` → *USA*, `PS` → *Palästina*) and the institutions `EU` (*Europäische
Union / European Union / Union européenne / Unión Europea*) and `UN`. ICU has **no demonyms and no capitals**; those
would need Wikidata (P1549 demonym, P36 capital, CC0) through a pinned SPARQL query.

Whether either is worth having was measured first, by deleting today's hand-made country and capital entries:

| Variant (from develop) | Entries | Day net | Week net (new / lost) |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1: country and capital entries deleted (EU, UN, NATO and the news entries kept) | 5,407 / 29,429 | +2 | **+53** (104 / 51) |
| T2: news-specific entries deleted (Zelensky, WM, Kreml) | 5,407 / 29,429 | +2 | −19 (4 / 23) |
| T3: every entry deleted | 5,407 / 29,429 | +8 | −7 (125 / 132) |

**Today's country and capital entries cost 53 week pairs net.** The eye check of T1 shows why: joining *Russland /
Russia / Russie / Rusia* into one stem raises its document frequency and lowers its weight, and a stem that common
rarely decides a link; within one language Snowball already joins the inflections. Removing them reassembles real
stories (*Generalstreik in Spanien* 3+3 → 4, the Kraków attack 6+3 → 7, the Moscow envoy talks 13 → 14). EU, UN and
NATO together are worth about 41 week pairs (T1 → T3 minus T2). A generated, larger territory table would add more of
what measured as net negative, so it is not built (Decision D3).

### R3. The proper-noun guard

**German "known" fails on names.** The #2230 lexicon is the 290,000 most frequent book forms, and it holds the names
of the #2227 loss cases as nouns: *mike* (class 11), *stacy* 16, *manchester* 13, *united* 11, *booker* 17, *wenger*
14, *hammer* 11, also *trump*, *merz*, *macron*, *steinmeier*. A guard keyed on "not in the lexicon" never fires for
them, and they are exactly the German side of the loss (de `mik / hamm / manch / book / weng` against en `mike /
hammer / manchest / booker / wenger`).

**Precision of "capitalised and not sentence-initial"**, over the week (28,548 entries, titles and bodies; a text
with no lower-case content word, such as Title Case or an all-caps headline, counts as caseless and is skipped;
seeds skipped). "Changed" means the bare form differs from the stem, so the guard would do something:

| Language | Caseless texts | Changed tokens | Distinct forms | Forms never written lower-case in the window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| en | 3,551 of 30,643 | 21,224 | 4,513 | 32 % of the tokens |
| de | 2,283 of 20,107 | 43,698 | 11,899 | meaningless (every noun is capitalised); 87 % of the tokens are lexicon forms |
| fr | 38 of 597 | 646 | 316 | 89 % |
| es | 69 of 1,296 | 1,468 | 786 | 76 % |

The most frequent English hits are institutional words: *House, Democrats, President, League, Senate, University,
City, States, Nations, National, Department, Party*. Two thirds of the English tokens it would change belong to
forms also written lower-case. No permissive en/fr/es word list is on the machine or in the dependencies; the macOS
`/usr/share/dict/words` (Webster's 1934, public domain) is English only, archaic and absent on Linux CI.

Two designs were prototyped and measured anyway (scratch copies of `HeadlineEquivalents` and `HeadlineWindow`, with
the word's case kept from the raw text):

| Prototype | Entries | Day net | Week net (new / lost) | Week cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Replace:** a capitalised non-initial word keeps its bare form instead of its stem (en/fr/es always; German only when the lexicon lacks it) | 5,470 / 29,480 | −30 | **−1,236** (860 / 2,096) | about even |
| **B. Add:** the bare form joins the word's stems as an extra part, counted through the #2230 part rules R6/R8, in every language | 5,455 / 29,472 | +42 | **−45** (253 / 298) | +11 % time (69.4 → 77.2 s), peak PHP memory 178.6 → 229.3 MB |

A splits *President* from *president* and *House* from *house*, as the precision predicted. B joins the cross-language
names it was meant to (the German *Dana White* piece joins the English group), but the new cross-language links
re-shuffle large English groups (*Jensen Huang*, *Eisenkot*) and lose more pairs than they gain, for 51 MB of memory.
Neither is built (Decision D5).

**Macht / machen.** In the week window German has *macht* 226 times, *Macht* 52, *Mächte* 3, *machen* 204. Snowball
gives *Macht, macht, Mächte, machte* the stem `macht` and *machen* `mach` (a seed now, D1). Keeping the noun *Macht*
apart from the verb *macht* needs a noun-aware stem for capitalised German words, and that splits *Ukraine* from
*ukrainische* and *Deutsche* from *deutsche* (the demonym class #2227 measured at 66 week pairs). Telling a verb from
an adjective would need part-of-speech data beyond the lexicon's noun/other split. Not built (Decision D6).

### R4. Transliteration

T2 above deletes every Zelensky spelling, `WM` and `Kreml`: −19 week pairs, +2 day. The losses are the English
*Zelensky / Zelenskyy* pair inside one language (Snowball English gives `zelenski` and `zelenskyy`), two German
*Selenskyj* posts, and German *WM* groups. *WM* has two letters, below the three-letter minimum, so it drops out once its
exact-word entry goes. In the week window the spellings occur as en *Zelenskyy* 81, *Zelensky* 18; de *Selenskyj* 28,
*Selenskij* 2; fr *Zelensky* 6; es *Zelenski* 5. With no guard (R3), nothing else changes that number. A fold that
rewrites *z/s, w/v, ck/k, -yj/-yy/-iy* for every word would buy back at most these 19 pairs (0.1 % of the week), at
the risk of new collisions in all four languages. Not built (Decision D7).

### Combined check

The two changes this spec builds, measured together before planning (C = Snowball seeds and the tables cut to EU, UN
and NATO, against develop, both on 5,495 day and 29,502 week entries): **day +31** (83 / 52), **week +540** (912 /
372), week wall time 67.9 → 64.7 s, peak PHP memory 179.1 → 177.7 MB. The largest new and grown groups are real
stories joined more completely (the FAA ground stop, Van Hollen against Schumer, Dana White, the Russell Brand trial,
the Brooklyn collapse, the *Fall Fabian* verdict, *SS United States*). The measurement task repeats this on the built
code.

## Approaches considered

Seeds:
1. **Snowball lists only.** Chosen for German and English.
2. Snowball ∪ today's words. Rejected: the extra words are hand-picked and worth 13 week pairs.
3. `stopwords-iso` (MIT). Rejected: its lists run to several hundred words per language and include content words; a
   seed is dropped outright, so a content word in the list is a silent loss.
4. **Snowball ∪ a closed-class supplement for French and Spanish**, derived by a part-of-speech rule from Google Books
   Ngram v3 (CC BY 3.0). Chosen in the fix wave (D10). Jacques Savoy's French and Spanish lists (unine.ch) were also
   considered; their page states no licence, so they are not used.

Territories:
1. **None; delete the hand-made entries.** Chosen (R2).
2. ICU names in four languages, one canonical per region code; ICU is offline and Unicode-licensed. Rejected by the
   measurement.
3. ICU plus Wikidata demonyms and capitals. Rejected by the same measurement, and it adds a networked generator.

Proper nouns:
1. **No guard.** Chosen (R3).
2. Replace the stem by the bare form (A). Rejected: −1,236 week pairs.
3. Add the bare form as a part (B). Rejected: −45 week pairs, +51 MB.
4. A guard backed by Google Books Ngram v3 en/fr/es 1-grams (CC BY 3.0), using each form's capitalised share. Not
   tried: it needs tens of GB of downloads, and B already shows that the cross-language links a perfect name detector
   would add do not pay on this data.

## Design

### Stopword data

`backend/resources/stopwords/` (named `snowball-stopwords/` until the fix wave added a second source):

- `generate.php` fetches the four `stop.txt` files from
  `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/snowballstem/snowball-website/<commit>/algorithms/<name>/stop.txt` at the commit
  pinned in a constant, and aborts unless each body's SHA-256 matches the one pinned beside it. Per line it drops the
  `|` comment and whitespace, keeps the first token, casefolds it (`MB_CASE_FOLD`, then NFKC, diacritics kept), and
  keeps it only if it matches `^\p{L}+$`. This drops the 50 English contractions. The tokenizer would split *doesn't*
  into `doesn` and `t`, and keep the head as a content word, so dropping them is a choice and not a no-op: keeping the
  heads would make *won* and *don* seeds. It removes duplicates, sorts each list byte-wise, and writes `stopwords.php`.
- For French and Spanish, `generate.php` also reads the Google Books Ngram v3 1-gram shards (D10) from a directory
  given as its argument, checks each shard's pinned SHA-256, and adds every form that is among the 1,000 most
  frequent by tagged count over 1950–2019 and holds at least half its tagged count in ADP, ADV, CONJ, DET, PRON or PRT.
  Each language's list is the sorted, distinct union of both sources.
- `stopwords.php` is generated, never edited by hand:
  `return ['de' => list<string>, 'en' => list<string>, 'fr' => list<string>, 'es' => list<string>];`
  It is a few kilobytes; it is marked `linguist-generated=true -diff` in `.gitattributes`.
- `SOURCE` pins the repository, the commit and its date, the four URLs with their SHA-256, the fetch date, the parse
  rule, the word counts, and the end-to-end reproduction (generated twice, `cmp` identical).
- `LICENSE-BSD-3-Clause.txt` is the 3-clause BSD text as snowballstem.org states it (© 2001 Dr Martin Porter, © 2002
  Richard Boulton); `LICENSE-CC-BY-3.0.txt` is the CC BY 3.0 legal code. `NOTICE.txt` names both sources, their
  licences and the changes, and states that the Snowball words are BSD, the Ngram-derived words CC BY 3.0, and the
  repository's code MIT.

### Runtime

In `Service/Curation/Trending`:

- **`StopWords/StopWordsInterface`** — `wordsOf(FeedLanguage $language): list<string>`.
- **`StopWords/BundledStopWords`** — `final`, constructed with
  `#[Autowire('%kernel.project_dir%/resources/stopwords/stopwords.php')] string $path`. `wordsOf` requires the file on
  first use, keeps it until `reset()` (`ResetInterface`), and returns that language's list; a file that is not the
  expected shape throws `\UnexpectedValueException`.
- **`HeadlineFiller`** takes `HeadlineStemmer` and `StopWordsInterface`. Its constructor builds, per language, the set
  of `stem(word, language)` over that language's stopwords (as today), and the set for a word without a language: the
  union over the four languages of `HeadlineStems::cut(word)` for each kept word. `SEED_WORDS` and `SEED_STEMS` are
  deleted. `isSeed`, `isWindowFiller`, `isFeedFiller` and `rarityWeight` keep their signatures and behaviour.
- **`HeadlineEquivalents`** keeps its code and its five tables, cut to the EU, UN and NATO rows listed in R5:
  - `EXACT_WORDS`: `EU`, `UE` → `eunion`; `UN`, `UNO`, `ONU` → `onu`;
  - `SHOUTABLE_WORDS`: `UN`, `UNO`;
  - `PHRASES`: the five UN and five EU phrases;
  - `CUT_STEMS`: `otan` → `nato`;
  - `STEMS_BY_LANGUAGE`: `de` and `en` empty, `fr` and `es` `otan` → `nato`.
  The `CUT_STEMS` docblock loses its instruction to check the window's words. The class docblock says once, in one
  line, which entries belong: institutions whose names do not change with the news, never a person, an event or a
  country (#2231).

Nothing else changes: `HeadlineWindow`, `HeadlineStemmer`, the splitter, the weights and the thresholds are untouched.

## Decisions

Lars delegated every decision. Each is "what — why — cost if wrong".

- **D1. Seeds are the Snowball lists alone** — they are the standard news-independent source for exactly these four
  languages, BSD-licensed, and measured +504 week / +29 day; the words only today's list has are worth 13 week pairs.
  — If wrong: frequent words such as en *will, can, also, just* link entries in a small window until the 3 %
  window-filler share catches them; about 13 week pairs on this data. French and Spanish get a supplement (D10).
- **D2. A word without a language seeds on the union of the four lists, cut to six letters** — it is the rule today's
  `SEED_STEMS` followed (all four languages' words, cut), derived from the same data; language-less entries are 1.3 %
  of the week window (383 of 28,548), and the ones sampled had an empty or emoji-only title. — If wrong: a language-less post loses words that are
  function words in one language and content words in another (*war, son, con, sin, fin*), which #2225 had
  excluded by hand; the tests that pinned those exclusions change. Worse, a seed of six letters or more also swallows
  every token whose six-letter cut it equals: `manche` (*manches*) takes *Manchester*, `diesel` (*dieselben*) *Diesel*,
  `sonder` (*sondern*) *Sondervermögen*, `contra` *contract*, `estado` *estadounidense*, `themse` (*themselves*)
  *Themse*, `durant` *Durant*, `wahren` (*während*) *wahren*; the D10 supplement adds `direct` (*directement*),
  `genera` (*généralement*), `partic` (*particulièrement*), `simple` (*simplement*), `precis` (*precisamente*) and
  `concer` (*concernant*). Only language-less entries are cut, 1.3 % of the week, so this is accepted.
- **D3. No territory data; the country and capital entries are deleted** — they measured −53 week pairs net (T1), and
  a generated table would only add more of the same. — If wrong: cross-language country links (*Polen / Poland*)
  are lost; on this data that is a gain, and the measurement task re-checks it.
- **D4. EU, UN and NATO stay hand-written** — they are institutions whose names have not changed in decades, so they are
  news-independent; they are worth about 41 week pairs; generating eight phrases from ICU would add a generator for
  the same rows, and ICU has no NATO. — If wrong: three hand-made rows remain; the rule against per-name entries is
  untouched because none of them is a person or an event.
- **D5. No proper-noun guard** — the German lexicon contains the names, so "not a known word" cannot find them; a
  replacing guard loses 1,236 week pairs; an additive one loses 45 week pairs and costs 51 MB. — If wrong: the
  cross-language name loss #2227 estimated (about 104 week pairs, mostly one *Mike Hammer* / *Stacy Keach* story)
  stays.
- **D6. *Macht* / *machen* stays as it is** — keeping the noun apart from the verb would split nouns from their
  adjectives (*Ukraine / ukrainische*), and the lexicon cannot tell verbs from adjectives; *machen* becomes a seed under
  D1 anyway. — If wrong: *Macht* (52 a week) keeps sharing a stem with the verb *macht* (226).
- **D7. No transliteration fold** — deleting the Zelensky entries costs at most 19 week pairs, and a fold applies to
  every word in four languages. — If wrong: spellings of one name in different languages or transliterations stay
  apart; about 10 week pairs on this data.
- **D8. `WM` is not replaced by an acronym rule** — a general rule (keep a two-letter all-caps word outside a shouted
  run) would bring *WM* back, but it is optional scope: a few lines, a new collision class (*AI, KI, US*), and at most
  about 10 week pairs. — If wrong: two-letter acronyms other than EU and UN stay below the three-letter minimum, as they
  are today.
- **D10. French and Spanish seeds add a closed-class supplement from Google Books Ngram (fix wave)** — Snowball's
  French list holds pronouns, articles and the forms of *être* and *avoir*, but no prepositions or adverbs (*après,
  contre, depuis, entre, selon, très, plus*); Spanish lacks *tras, según*. These are closed-class and
  news-independent. A window of French or Spanish entries alone, as on an instance whose readers mostly read those
  languages, shows they are not harmless as content words: *après* reached 104 % of the filler share and *plus* 93 %
  in the French week, *tras* 129 % in the Spanish week, and the French-only week lost 6 pairs and gained 3 against
  develop. The rule is general and comes from a news-independent corpus: a form among the 1,000 most frequent book
  forms whose part-of-speech tags are at least half closed-class. It adds 123 French and 108 Spanish words. CC BY 3.0,
  as the German lexicon (#2230). — If wrong: a few homographs of content words become seeds (fr *mal, ensemble,
  dessus, son*; es *mal, frente, tarde, bajo, propio*), and the generator needs 5.3 GB of shards to reproduce.
- **D9. The stopwords ship as a generated PHP array behind an interface** — PHP needs no parse step for a few KB, and
  the interface follows the `GermanLexicon/` precedent; the test wiring passes the real file because it is small. —
  If wrong: one extra file and one extra class; nothing at runtime.

## Measurement plan

The #2227 method, as in #2230:

- A = develop at 75048da7e, B = this branch's HEAD; each variant's whole `src/Service/Curation/Trending` and
  `resources/stopwords` swapped in, the container warmed before each timed run.
- "Now" pinned to 2026-11-15 00:00:00 UTC (`simp-explain-at.php`), day and week, dev MySQL read-only.
- A and B back to back. Their header lines must show the same entry count; if the worker added back-dated entries in
  between, run A again and compare the pair with equal counts.
- Report net grouped pairs (new / lost), groups, wall time, peak RSS and peak PHP memory, and the largest new, grown
  and split groups, checked by eye for wrong merges and lost stories.

## Limitations

- **Snowball seeds a few nouns' and names' stems**, none of which the old hand list seeded. Accepted as Snowball's
  call; a table row is not the fix:
  - de: *Weg* (`weg`), *Sein* (`sein`), *Manchester* (`manch`, through *manche*), *Waren* (goods, `war`), *Wille /
    Willen* (`will`), *Weite* (`weit`, through *weiter*), *Anden* (`and`, through *anders*);
  - en: *Downing* (`down`), *Doe* (`doe`, through *does*);
  - fr: *été* (summer, `ete`, through *êtes*), *Mons* (`mon`);
  - es: *Estado / Estados* (`estad`), *era*, *sobre* (envelope), *fuera* (the verb and preposition forms).
  So two Downing Street posts lose *Downing*, two posts on Manchester United link only through *united*, and a Spanish
  *Estados Unidos* group links only through `unid`.
- **Two-letter acronyms** other than EU and UN, such as *WM, EM, KI, AI*, are below the three-letter minimum (D8).
- **Names across stemmers** stay split (D5): *Hammer* is `hamm` in German and `hammer` in English.
- **The closed-class supplement seeds homographs** (D10): fr *mal, ensemble, dessus, son, bien*; es *mal, frente,
  tarde, bajo, propio, bien*, and the number words of both languages.
