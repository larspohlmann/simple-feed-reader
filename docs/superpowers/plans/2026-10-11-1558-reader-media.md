# #1558 Reader media: feed-video handling gets one home

**Goal:** `reader/media/` holds the media plumbing both surfaces share — the embed upgrade and its
generated allow-list, the HLS streams and the unplayable-video recovery — and HLS gets an
explicit, host-owned lifetime. `article/decorators` keeps only the steps `decorateArticle` runs.
Behaviour unchanged.

## Rulings

- Pin first: `decorate-article.spec.ts` plays a cinema-wrapped `.m3u8` video after the real
  pipeline and expects hls.js to attach. Committed green on develop's code, then refactored with
  it in place.
- `reader/media/` takes `media-embeds.ts` (+ spec), `embed-frame-allowlist.generated.json`
  (the backend dump command, `.prettierignore` and the README follow), `hls-streams.ts` and
  `unplayable-videos.ts` (+ specs). `embed-frame.directive.ts` stays: it is a template directive,
  not media plumbing, and the issue does not name it.
- HLS lifetime: streams are registered under the host that armed them (`WeakMap` host → its
  hls.js instances), not in one global map. `armHlsStreams(host)` arms on render and drops that
  host's instances whose video left; `releaseHlsStreams(host)` destroys all of the host's
  instances. Rendering the list no longer sweeps the article's leftovers, or the reverse.
- The article releases from the cleanup of `ArticleBody`'s decoration effect, which runs only on
  a content swap (new HTML or a new container) and on destroy — never under a playing video.
  `decorateArticle` keeps arming as its last step, so the #1562 pin still covers the hand-off.
- "Would hls.js take this video?" becomes `streamsThroughHls(video)`: a playlist `src`, or a
  video already armed (its `src` is a `blob:` once hls.js attached). It answers before decoration
  runs, so the fallback no longer depends on decorator order.
- The video fallback becomes an injectable `UnplayableVideos` in `reader/media/` that builds its
  own labels from Transloco; `ArticleBody` calls it beside `ImageProxyService.recover` and keeps
  no label closure.
- The older-iOS armed-but-unsupported gap: the only pre-17.1 runtime here (iOS 16.0) is
  unavailable on this macOS, so it cannot be checked; the exemption keeps today's semantics and
  the gap is reported, not changed.
