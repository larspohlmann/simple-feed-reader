Kambo Is More Dangerous Than You Think

The risks of this Amazonian frog ritual are well documented, and the deaths below are drawn from published medical case reports rather than rumour, so this opening block is deliberately long and prose heavy to clear the extraction's character threshold on its own without any navigation.

Natasha Lechner, 39, Australia, 2019

Died shortly after a Kambo ceremony. Medical experts concluded that an acute coronary event was the likely cause, and the coroner's report set out the sequence of collapse in enough clinical detail to fill a substantial paragraph of ordinary readable prose.

Jarrad Antonovich, 46, Australia, 2021

Died after taking Kambo at a festival. His esophagus ruptured following severe and repeated vomiting, a rare but recognised complication that the inquest examined at length across several ordinary sentences of readable prose that comfortably exceed the length gate on their own.

The Danger Is Part of the Intended Effect

What makes this ritual uniquely concerning is that one of its most serious risks arises not because something goes wrong, but because the substance does exactly what it is supposed to do, and that intended effect is itself the mechanism of harm in these documented cases.
